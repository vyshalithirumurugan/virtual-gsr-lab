SIMULATION MODE
01 / EXPERIMENT OBJECTIVE

Aim

◎

Measurement of Galvanic Skin Resistance

To measure basal and actual galvanic skin resistance at rest and under mild mental or physical stimuli, and to observe changes in skin resistance during stress and relaxation.

Learning outcomes

  • Understand the principle of GSR measurement.
  • Differentiate basal and actual skin resistance.
  • Observe resistance changes during stress and relaxation stimuli.
  • Interpret the simulated biofeedback response.
02 / FUNDAMENTALS

Theory

What is Galvanic Skin Resistance?

GSR is the electrical resistance of the skin. It is influenced by sweat gland activity, which is controlled by the autonomic nervous system. Changes in emotional or physiological arousal can alter sweating and therefore change the electrical properties of the skin.

Measurement principle

A small constant DC current is applied through electrodes placed on the skin. The voltage across the skin is measured. Using Ohm's law, resistance is calculated as R = V / I.

Stress and relaxation

Increased sweating generally increases skin conductance and decreases skin resistance. Relaxation and reduced sweat gland activity may increase skin resistance.

Basal and actual resistance

Basal skin resistance is measured under resting conditions and provides a reference level. Actual skin resistance is observed during or after a stimulus, allowing changes from baseline to be studied.

The virtual trace is illustrative. Real GSR recordings depend on electrode contact, skin condition, environment and instrument settings.

05 / EXPERIMENT METHOD

Procedure

Experimental procedure

  1. Prepare the subject.

    Allow the subject to sit comfortably and explain the procedure.

  2. Prepare the electrodes.

    Clean the skin and place the finger electrodes in the recommended positions.

  3. Record basal resistance.

    Allow the subject to rest and note the basal skin resistance value.

  4. Record actual resistance.

    Observe the resistance while applying a mild mental or physical stimulus.

  5. Apply different stimuli.

    Observe responses to mental arithmetic, sudden sound, deep breathing, cool water or guided relaxation.

  6. Compare the readings.

    Compare the actual response with the basal value and note the direction of change.

  7. Record observations.

    Enter the values and response pattern in the observation table.
